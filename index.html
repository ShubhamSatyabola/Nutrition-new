Importance of Biochemistry in Nursing
Introduction
👆 Click to expand notes

Definition

  • Biochemistry = study of chemical processes within and related to living organisms
  • Also called biological chemistry or physiological chemistry

Why Nurses Need Biochemistry

  • Understand normal vs abnormal lab values (blood glucose, urea, proteins)
  • Interpret diagnostic tests — LFT, RFT, lipid profile, enzyme assays
  • Understand drug action at molecular level (pharmacokinetics & pharmacodynamics)
  • Manage IV fluids, electrolyte therapy, TPN (Total Parenteral Nutrition)
  • Understand disease pathogenesis — diabetes, gout, jaundice, anemia
  • Handle & store specimens correctly for accurate enzyme estimation

Clinical Relevance

  • Blood pH monitoring — acid-base balance in ICU patients
  • Urine analysis — ketones, proteins, glucose
  • Nutritional assessment based on biochemical parameters
🔬
Cell Structure & Components
Cell Biology
👆 Click to expand notes

Cell Definition

  • Fundamental unit of life; all living organisms are made of cells
  • Human body has ~37 trillion cells

Major Cell Components

  • Cell membrane: Phospholipid bilayer — selectively permeable; contains proteins & cholesterol
  • Nucleus: Contains DNA (genetic material); bounded by nuclear envelope; site of transcription
  • Cytoplasm: Gel-like matrix between membrane & nucleus; site of many metabolic reactions
  • Mitochondria: "Powerhouse of cell" — ATP synthesis via oxidative phosphorylation
  • Ribosomes: Site of protein synthesis; free (cytoplasmic) or bound (ER)
  • Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): RER — protein processing; SER — lipid synthesis & detox
  • Golgi apparatus: Packaging & secretion of proteins; post-translational modification
  • Lysosomes: Contain hydrolytic enzymes; intracellular digestion
  • Peroxisomes: Oxidative reactions; breakdown of fatty acids; H₂O₂ metabolism
  • Cytoskeleton: Microfilaments, microtubules, intermediate filaments — structural support
🧠 Mnemonic: "My Neighbour Came Running Every Gloomy Lovely Rainy PM" — Membrane, Nucleus, Cytoplasm, Ribosomes, ER, Golgi, Lysosomes, RER, Peroxisomes, Mitochondria

Water — Properties & Functions in the Body
Water
👆 Click to expand notes

Sources of Water

  • Exogenous: drinking water, beverages, food (~2000 ml/day)
  • Endogenous (metabolic water): oxidation of nutrients (~300 ml/day)

Physical Properties

  • High specific heat capacity — resists temperature change; body temperature regulation
  • High latent heat of vaporization — evaporation cools body (sweating)
  • Universal solvent — polar molecule dissolves ions, proteins, gases
  • High surface tension — important in alveoli (surfactant counteracts)
  • Ionization: H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻ (Kw = 10⁻¹⁴)

Functions in Human Body

  • Transport medium for nutrients, gases, waste products
  • Reactant in hydrolysis reactions & metabolic processes
  • Temperature regulation via sweating & insulation
  • Lubricant — synovial fluid, pleural fluid, CSF
  • Cushioning — amniotic fluid, CSF (shock absorber)
  • Maintains blood volume & blood pressure
🧠 Functions: TRLLTM — Transport, Reactant, Lubrication, Lytic reactions, Temperature, Metabolite
⚖️
Water & Fluid Balance
Fluid Balance
👆 Click to expand notes

Body Fluid Compartments

  • Total Body Water (TBW): ~60% of body weight in males; ~50% in females
  • Intracellular Fluid (ICF): 40% body weight; K⁺ rich; major electrolyte = K⁺
  • Extracellular Fluid (ECF): 20% body weight; Na⁺ rich; includes plasma & interstitial fluid

Daily Water Balance (Intake = Output)

  • Intake: Fluids ~1500 ml + Food ~700 ml + Metabolic ~300 ml = ~2500 ml
  • Output: Urine ~1500 ml + Insensible loss (skin/lung) ~900 ml + Feces ~100 ml = ~2500 ml

Regulation

  • ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone) — increases water reabsorption in collecting duct
  • Aldosterone — promotes Na⁺ & water retention, K⁺ excretion
  • Atrial Natriuretic Peptide (ANP) — promotes Na⁺ & water excretion
  • Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) — regulates blood volume

Nursing Implications

  • Monitor I&O chart strictly in ICU/post-op patients
  • Dehydration signs: dry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor, oliguria
  • Overhydration: edema, dyspnea, hypertension

Electrolytes of the Human Body
Electrolytes
👆 Click to expand notes

Sodium (Na⁺) — Normal: 135–145 mEq/L

  • Major ECF cation; regulates osmolality & blood volume
  • Sources: table salt, processed foods
  • Hyponatremia: confusion, seizures; Hypernatremia: thirst, dry skin

Potassium (K⁺) — Normal: 3.5–5.0 mEq/L

  • Major ICF cation; essential for nerve impulse & muscle contraction (cardiac!)
  • Sources: bananas, oranges, potatoes
  • Hypokalemia: muscle weakness, arrhythmia; Hyperkalemia: cardiac arrest risk

Calcium (Ca²⁺) — Normal: 8.5–10.5 mg/dL

  • Bone formation, muscle contraction, nerve function, blood clotting
  • Hypocalcemia: tetany (Chvostek & Trousseau signs); Hypercalcemia: renal stones

Magnesium (Mg²⁺) — Normal: 1.5–2.5 mEq/L

  • Cofactor for 300+ enzymes; ATP metabolism; neuromuscular function

Chloride (Cl⁻) — Normal: 98–106 mEq/L

  • Major ECF anion; HCl in gastric juice; maintains acid-base balance

Phosphate (PO₄³⁻)

  • Bone mineralization; ATP formation; buffer system
🧠 Cation memory: "Na⁺ is OUT, K⁺ is IN" — Sodium = ECF, Potassium = ICF

Enzymes — Mechanism of Action
Enzymes
👆 Click to expand notes

Definition & Properties

  • Biological catalysts — protein in nature (except ribozymes = RNA enzymes)
  • Speed up chemical reactions without being consumed
  • Highly specific — one enzyme, one substrate (lock & key / induced fit)

Mechanism — Lock & Key Theory

  • Active site of enzyme has specific shape complementary to substrate
  • Substrate binds → Enzyme-Substrate (ES) complex forms → Products released → Enzyme regenerated
  • E + S → ES → E + P

Mechanism — Induced Fit Theory (Koshland)

  • Active site is flexible; it changes shape upon substrate binding
  • More accurate model explaining enzyme specificity

Enzyme Nomenclature

  • IUB system: 6 classes — Oxidoreductases, Transferases, Hydrolases, Lyases, Isomerases, Ligases
  • Named with "-ase" suffix e.g., amylase, lipase, protease

Coenzymes & Cofactors

  • Cofactors: inorganic (metal ions — Zn²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺)
  • Coenzymes: organic (vitamins — NAD⁺, FAD, CoA)
  • Apoenzyme + Cofactor = Holoenzyme (active)
🌡️
Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity
Enzymes
👆 Click to expand notes

Temperature

  • Optimum: 37°C (body temperature) for most human enzymes
  • ↑ temperature → ↑ activity (up to optimum)
  • Above 40–45°C → denaturation → irreversible loss of activity

pH

  • Optimum pH varies: Pepsin = 1.5–2.5; Trypsin = 7.5–8.0; Salivary amylase = 6.8–7.0
  • Extreme pH alters ionization of active site → reduced activity

Substrate Concentration

  • Michaelis-Menten kinetics: Km = substrate conc. at half Vmax
  • Low Km = high affinity for substrate

Inhibitors

  • Competitive: Structurally similar to substrate; blocks active site (reversed by ↑[S])
  • Non-competitive: Binds allosteric site; changes enzyme shape; NOT reversed by ↑[S]
  • Clinical: Aspirin irreversibly inhibits COX; statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase

Activators

  • Metal ions (Mg²⁺ activates kinases), Cl⁻ activates salivary amylase
🧫
Diagnostic Applications of Enzymes
Diagnostics
👆 Click to expand notes

Cardiac Enzymes

  • CPK (CK-MB): Rises 4–6 hrs post-MI, peaks 24 hrs — most specific for MI
  • LDH (LDH-1): Rises 24–48 hrs, peaks 3–6 days
  • Troponin I & T: Gold standard — rises 3–4 hrs, stays elevated 2 weeks

Liver Enzymes

  • AST (SGOT): Liver, heart, muscle — raised in hepatitis, MI
  • ALT (SGPT): More specific to liver — best indicator of liver damage
  • ALP: Liver & bone; raised in cholestasis, bone disease
  • GGT: Sensitive marker for alcohol-related liver disease

Pancreatic Enzymes

  • Serum amylase: Rises within 6 hrs in acute pancreatitis (falls by 2–3 days)
  • Serum lipase: More specific than amylase; stays elevated longer (7–10 days)

Other

  • PSA (Prostatic acid phosphatase): Prostatic carcinoma
  • ACE: Elevated in sarcoidosis
🧪
Precautions for Handling Enzyme Estimation Specimens
Specimen Handling
👆 Click to expand notes

Collection Precautions

  • Collect blood WITHOUT anticoagulant for most enzyme tests (serum preferred)
  • Avoid hemolysis — RBCs contain LDH, AST, ALT; hemolyzed samples give false high values
  • Use correct vacutainer — red top (serum) for cardiac enzymes
  • Collect at correct time — cardiac enzymes have a temporal pattern post-MI

Storage & Transport

  • Analyse immediately or refrigerate at 4°C (most enzymes stable 24–48 hrs at 4°C)
  • Do NOT freeze and thaw repeatedly — denatures enzymes
  • LDH is unstable at 4°C — store at room temperature or analyse within 2 hrs
  • Protect from light (some enzymes are photosensitive)

Patient Preparation

  • Avoid IM injections before CPK estimation (raises CPK due to muscle trauma)
  • Fasting required for lipase/amylase in some protocols
  • Note patient medications (statins, steroids affect enzyme levels)

Digestion & Absorption — Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats
Digestion
👆 Click to expand notes

Carbohydrate Digestion

  • Mouth: Salivary amylase → starch → maltose + dextrins (pH 6.8)
  • Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase → continued breakdown
  • Brush border enzymes: maltase → glucose; sucrase → glucose + fructose; lactase → glucose + galactose
  • Absorption: active transport (SGLT1 for glucose & galactose), facilitated (GLUT5 for fructose)

Protein Digestion

  • Stomach: Pepsinogen → Pepsin (HCl activates); cleaves large proteins
  • Pancreas: Trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, carboxypeptidase
  • Brush border: Dipeptidases, aminopeptidases → amino acids
  • Absorption: amino acid transporters (Na⁺-dependent) into enterocytes → portal vein

Fat Digestion

  • Mouth: Lingual lipase (minor role)
  • Stomach: Gastric lipase
  • Small intestine (main): Bile salts emulsify fats → ↑ surface area for lipase
  • Pancreatic lipase → triglycerides → 2-monoglyceride + 2 fatty acids
  • Absorption: micelles → passive diffusion into enterocytes → chylomicrons → lymphatics (lacteals)

Malabsorption Syndrome

  • Causes: Celiac disease (gluten-sensitive), Crohn's disease, lactase deficiency, cystic fibrosis
  • Features: Steatorrhea (fatty stools), weight loss, nutritional deficiencies, diarrhea
  • Nursing: Monitor weight, stool characteristics, nutritional supplements, dietary modifications

Catabolism of Carbohydrates for Energy
Metabolism
👆 Click to expand notes

Overview of Pathways

  • Glycolysis → Pyruvate → Acetyl-CoA → TCA cycle → ETC → ATP
  • Aerobic: 38 ATP per glucose; Anaerobic: 2 ATP per glucose

Glycolysis (Cytoplasm)

  • Glucose (6C) → 2 Pyruvate (3C) + 2 ATP + 2 NADH
  • 10 enzyme-catalyzed steps; net gain = 2 ATP
  • Rate-limiting enzyme: Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)
  • Occurs in cytoplasm; does NOT require oxygen

Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex

  • Pyruvate → Acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH (mitochondria)
  • Irreversible — "one-way gate" into TCA cycle
  • Requires: TPP (B1), Lipoic acid, CoA (B5), FAD (B2), NAD⁺ (B3)

TCA Cycle / Krebs Cycle (Mitochondrial matrix)

  • Acetyl-CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) → Citrate (6C) → ... → OAA
  • Per cycle: 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 GTP + 2 CO₂
  • Rate-limiting enzyme: Isocitrate dehydrogenase
🧠 TCA intermediates: "Citrate Is Krebs' Starting Substrate For Making Oxaloacetate" — Citrate, Isocitrate, α-Ketoglutarate, Succinyl-CoA, Succinate, Fumarate, Malate, Oxaloacetate
⚡
Mitochondrial Oxidation & Oxidative Phosphorylation
Bioenergetics
👆 Click to expand notes

Electron Transport Chain (ETC)

  • Located in inner mitochondrial membrane
  • 4 complexes (I, II, III, IV) + ATP synthase (Complex V)
  • NADH → Complex I; FADH₂ → Complex II (bypasses complex I → fewer ATP)
  • Electrons flow → ½O₂ + 2H⁺ → H₂O at Complex IV

Chemiosmosis (Mitchell's hypothesis)

  • H⁺ pumped into intermembrane space creating proton gradient
  • H⁺ flow back through ATP synthase → ADP + Pi → ATP
  • NADH yields ~2.5 ATP; FADH₂ yields ~1.5 ATP

Total ATP from 1 Glucose

  • Glycolysis: 2 ATP + 2 NADH (= 5 ATP)
  • PDH: 2 NADH (= 5 ATP)
  • TCA: 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 GTP (= ~18 ATP)
  • Total ≈ 30–32 ATP

Inhibitors (Clinical)

  • Cyanide: blocks Complex IV → tissue hypoxia
  • Metformin: inhibits Complex I → reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis
  • Carbon monoxide: binds hemoglobin & Complex IV

Fate & Storage of Glucose — Glycogen Metabolism
Glucose Metabolism
👆 Click to expand notes

Glycogenesis (Glucose → Glycogen)

  • Occurs in liver & muscle; triggered by insulin (post-meal)
  • Glucose → Glucose-6-P → Glucose-1-P → UDP-Glucose → Glycogen
  • Key enzyme: Glycogen synthase
  • Liver stores ~100g; Muscle ~400g glycogen

Glycogenolysis (Glycogen → Glucose)

  • Triggered by glucagon (fasting) & epinephrine (stress)
  • Glycogen → Glucose-1-P → Glucose-6-P → Glucose (liver only, via G6Pase)
  • Key enzyme: Glycogen phosphorylase

Gluconeogenesis (Non-carbohydrate → Glucose)

  • Occurs in liver (mainly) & kidneys during prolonged fasting
  • Substrates: lactate, glycerol, glucogenic amino acids (alanine, glutamine)
  • Regulated by glucagon, cortisol; inhibited by insulin
  • Key enzymes: PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, G6Pase, pyruvate carboxylase

Other Fates of Glucose

  • Pentose phosphate pathway — NADPH + ribose-5-P (nucleotide synthesis)
  • Lipogenesis — excess glucose → acetyl-CoA → fatty acids
  • Uronic acid pathway — glucuronate for detoxification
🩸
Blood Glucose Regulation & GTT
Clinical
👆 Click to expand notes

Normal Blood Glucose

  • Fasting: 70–100 mg/dL; 2-hr post-prandial: <140 mg/dL
  • Renal threshold for glucose: ~180 mg/dL (glycosuria above this)

Regulation

  • Insulin (↓ glucose): ↑ glycogenesis, ↑ glycolysis, ↑ lipogenesis, ↑ protein synthesis, ↓ gluconeogenesis
  • Glucagon (↑ glucose): ↑ glycogenolysis, ↑ gluconeogenesis (liver)
  • Other hyperglycemic hormones: Cortisol, GH, Epinephrine, Thyroid hormone

Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT)

  • 75g glucose orally; glucose measured at 0, 1, 2 hours
  • Normal: <140 mg/dL at 2 hr; Pre-DM: 140–199; DM: ≥200 mg/dL
  • Used to diagnose Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)

Hyper & Hypoglycemia

  • Hyperglycemia: DM type 1 (absolute insulin lack) or type 2 (insulin resistance)
  • Hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL): Excess insulin, missed meals, alcohol; symptoms: sweating, tremors, palpitations, confusion
  • HbA1c: Reflects average glucose over 2–3 months; Normal <5.7%; DM ≥6.5%

Amino Acids & Essential Amino Acids
Proteins
👆 Click to expand notes

Structure

  • Basic structure: NH₂–CHR–COOH (amino group + carboxyl group + R side chain)
  • 20 standard amino acids; classified by R group polarity, charge
  • Peptide bond: formed by condensation between –COOH and –NH₂

Essential Amino Acids (cannot be synthesized — must be eaten)

  • Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine*, Leucine, Lysine (*semi-essential)
🧠 Mnemonic: "PVT TIM HaLL" — Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine, Leucine, Lysine

Protein & Hormones

  • Protein hormones: Insulin, Glucagon, GH, PTH, ACTH, TSH — all peptide-based
  • Synthesized in ribosomes → processed in RER → secreted via Golgi
📜
Role of Nucleic Acids in Protein Synthesis
Molecular Biology
👆 Click to expand notes

Central Dogma

  • DNA → (Transcription) → mRNA → (Translation) → Protein

Types of RNA

  • mRNA: Carries genetic code from nucleus to ribosome (codons)
  • tRNA: Carries amino acids to ribosome (anticodon matches mRNA codon)
  • rRNA: Structural component of ribosome; catalytic function (ribozyme)

Transcription

  • DNA template → mRNA by RNA polymerase (in nucleus)
  • Requires promoter region, transcription factors

Translation

  • mRNA codon (3 bases) read by ribosome; tRNA brings corresponding amino acid
  • Start codon: AUG (methionine); Stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA
  • Polyribosomes: multiple ribosomes on one mRNA for efficiency

Post-translational Modification

  • Glycosylation, phosphorylation, cleavage of signal peptide
  • Insulin: preproinsulin → proinsulin → insulin (C-peptide removed)
♻️
Urea Cycle & Nitrogenous Compounds
Metabolism
👆 Click to expand notes

Urea Cycle (Krebs-Henseleit Cycle)

  • Occurs in liver; disposes of NH₃ (toxic) via urea (urine)
  • Steps: NH₃ + CO₂ → Carbamoyl phosphate → Citrulline → Argininosuccinate → Arginine → Urea + Ornithine
  • Ornithine is the carrier (regenerated); Fumarate feeds into TCA
🧠 "Ordinarily, Careless Crappers Are Also Frivolous About Urination" — Ornithine, Carbamoyl-P, Citrulline, Arginino-Succinate, Arginine, Fumarate, Aspartate, Urea

Nitrogenous Constituents of Blood

  • BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen): 8–20 mg/dL; ↑ in renal failure, dehydration
  • Creatinine: 0.6–1.2 mg/dL; more specific for renal function than BUN
  • Uric acid: 3.5–7.2 mg/dL; from purine catabolism

Uric Acid & Gout

  • Xanthine → Uric acid (by xanthine oxidase)
  • Hyperuricemia → urate crystals deposit in joints → gout (tophi in big toe)
  • Treatment: Allopurinol (inhibits xanthine oxidase), colchicine (acute attack)

Plasma Proteins

  • Albumin (60%): Maintains oncotic pressure, carrier protein, synthesized in liver; ↓ in liver disease
  • Globulins: α₁, α₂, β, γ-globulins — immune function, transport
  • Fibrinogen: Clotting cascade
  • Total protein normal: 6–8 g/dL; Albumin: 3.5–5.0 g/dL

Biosynthesis & Storage of Fats
Lipids
👆 Click to expand notes

Fatty Acid Synthesis (Lipogenesis)

  • Occurs in cytoplasm; requires NADPH (from pentose phosphate pathway)
  • Acetyl-CoA → Malonyl-CoA (by acetyl-CoA carboxylase — rate-limiting, requires biotin)
  • Fatty acid synthase (FAS) adds 2C units per cycle → palmitate (16C)
  • Stimulated by insulin; inhibited by glucagon, fasting

Triglyceride Synthesis & Storage

  • 3 fatty acids + glycerol-3-P → Triacylglycerol (TAG)
  • Stored in adipose tissue; mobilized by hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL)
  • HSL activated by: glucagon, epinephrine, cortisol; Inhibited by insulin

β-Oxidation of Fatty Acids

  • Fatty acids → Acetyl-CoA in mitochondria (requires carnitine shuttle)
  • Each cycle removes 2C as acetyl-CoA; produces NADH + FADH₂
  • Palmitate (16C) → 8 acetyl-CoA = 129 ATP total
🫀
Cholesterol, Lipoproteins & Liver Role
Cholesterol
👆 Click to expand notes

Role of Liver in Fat Metabolism

  • Synthesizes fatty acids, phospholipids, cholesterol, bile acids
  • Packages as VLDL for export; β-oxidation in fasting
  • Fatty liver (hepatic steatosis): excess fat accumulation in liver

Cholesterol

  • Precursor for: steroid hormones (cortisol, sex hormones), bile acids, vitamin D, cell membranes
  • Synthesized from acetyl-CoA; HMG-CoA reductase is rate-limiting enzyme
  • Normal total cholesterol: <200 mg/dL

Lipoproteins

  • Chylomicrons: From gut → lymph → blood; carry dietary fat
  • VLDL: From liver → peripheral tissues; carry endogenous TG
  • LDL ("bad"): Delivers cholesterol to tissues; ↑ → atherosclerosis (normal <100 mg/dL)
  • HDL ("good"): Reverse cholesterol transport to liver (normal >40 mg/dL)
🧠 LDL vs HDL: L = Lousy, H = Healthy
🔑
Ketone Bodies & Integrated Metabolism
Metabolism
👆 Click to expand notes

Ketone Bodies

  • Types: Acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, Acetone
  • Formed in liver from acetyl-CoA during fasting, DKA, starvation
  • Used as fuel by brain, heart, muscle (NOT liver)
  • DKA (Diabetic Ketoacidosis): insulin deficiency → ↑ lipolysis → ↑ ketones → metabolic acidosis
  • Signs: fruity breath (acetone), Kussmaul breathing, altered consciousness

Inter-relationships in Metabolism

  • Fed state: glucose → glycogen + fat (insulin dominant)
  • Fasting state: glycogenolysis → gluconeogenesis → ketogenesis (glucagon dominant)
  • Glucose-alanine cycle: muscle alanine → liver → gluconeogenesis → glucose back to muscle
  • Cori cycle: muscle lactate → liver → glucose (via gluconeogenesis) → muscle

Important Lipids

  • Phospholipids: cell membrane structure (lecithin, cephalin)
  • Sphingolipids: myelin sheath; storage disorders: Gaucher's, Niemann-Pick
  • Eicosanoids: prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes — inflammatory mediators
  • Essential fatty acids: Linoleic (ω-6), α-Linolenic (ω-3) — must come from diet

Concepts of Units & Measurements
Biophysics
👆 Click to expand notes

SI System (Système International)

  • International standard; 7 base units
  • Consistent globally — eliminates confusion in medical data

Fundamental Units

  • Length: metre (m)
  • Mass: kilogram (kg)
  • Time: second (s)
  • Temperature: Kelvin (K)
  • Electric current: Ampere (A)
  • Amount of substance: mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity: candela (cd)

Derived Units

  • Force: Newton (N) = kg·m/s²
  • Energy/Work: Joule (J) = N·m
  • Power: Watt (W) = J/s
  • Pressure: Pascal (Pa) = N/m²
  • Electric charge: Coulomb (C) = A·s
  • Frequency: Hertz (Hz) = cycles/s

Vector, Scalar, Speed, Velocity & Acceleration
Motion
👆 Click to expand notes

Scalar vs Vector Quantities

  • Scalar: Magnitude only — mass, time, temperature, distance, speed
  • Vector: Magnitude + Direction — velocity, force, acceleration, momentum

Speed vs Velocity

  • Speed: Rate of distance covered (scalar) — speed = distance/time
  • Velocity: Rate of displacement (vector) — velocity = displacement/time
  • Blood flow velocity in aorta: ~30–60 cm/s

Acceleration

  • Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt; unit: m/s²
  • Gravitational acceleration: g = 9.8 m/s²

Nursing Relevance

  • IV drip rate calculation uses flow rate (scalar — ml/hr)
  • Cardiac output — volume/time (L/min)
  • Peak flow meter — velocity of expired air

Gravity — Principles & Applications in Nursing
Gravity
👆 Click to expand notes

Specific Gravity

  • Ratio of density of substance to density of water (water = 1.000)
  • Urine specific gravity: 1.003–1.030 (concentrated urine → high SG)
  • Blood specific gravity: ~1.060; Plasma: ~1.025
  • Used in urinalysis, checking IV fluids, blood bank

Centre of Gravity

  • Point where entire weight of body acts; in humans = slightly anterior to S2
  • Lower CoG = more stable (wide base, bent knees)
  • Changes with posture, pregnancy, prosthetics

Effects of Gravitational Forces on Body

  • Orthostatic hypotension: standing → blood pools in legs → ↓ BP
  • Hydrostatic effect on venous return — leg elevation helps
  • Prolonged bed rest → muscle atrophy, bone demineralization

Nursing Applications

  • Trendelenburg position: head-down tilt → ↑ venous return (shock management)
  • Leg elevation: reduces edema, improves venous return
  • Urinary drainage bags always below bladder level (gravity drainage)
  • IV fluid flow depends on height of fluid bag above patient
  • Body mechanics training: bend knees, keep CoG low to prevent nurse back injuries

Force, Work & Energy — Units & Types
Mechanics
👆 Click to expand notes

Definitions

  • Force: Push or pull; F = ma; unit = Newton (N)
  • Work: W = F × d × cosθ; unit = Joule (J); done only when displacement occurs
  • Energy: Capacity to do work; unit = Joule (J)
  • Power: Rate of doing work; P = W/t; unit = Watt (W)

Types of Energy

  • Kinetic energy (KE): ½mv²; energy of motion
  • Potential energy (PE): mgh; stored energy (elevated position)
  • Chemical energy: stored in ATP, glucose, fats
  • Thermal energy: heat produced in metabolic reactions
  • Electrical energy: nerve conduction, ECG, EEG
  • Radiant energy: light, X-rays, UV radiation

Levers & Body Mechanics

  • Class 1 lever: Fulcrum between effort & load (seesaw, nodding head)
  • Class 2 lever: Load between fulcrum & effort (wheelbarrow, calf raise)
  • Class 3 lever: Most common in body; effort between fulcrum & load (bicep curl, jaw)

Friction & Inclined Plane in Nursing

  • Friction: opposes motion; reduced by lubricating joints (synovial fluid)
  • Inclined plane principle: ramps reduce effort needed to lift patients
  • Screw principle: spinal column — compression forces

Heat — Properties, Transfer & Temperature Scales
Heat
👆 Click to expand notes

Nature & Measurement

  • Heat = form of energy; measured in Joules (SI) or Calories (1 cal = 4.18 J)
  • Specific heat: heat needed to raise 1g substance by 1°C (water = 4.18 J/g·°C)
  • Latent heat of vaporization of water: 2260 J/g (basis of sweating cooling)

Modes of Heat Transfer

  • Conduction: Direct contact (hot water bottle; precaution: burns)
  • Convection: Fluid/air movement (air conditioning, body cooling)
  • Radiation: Electromagnetic waves (radiant warmers for neonates)
  • Evaporation: Sweat evaporation cools body (dominant at high temperature)

Temperature Scales

  • Celsius: 0°C (water freezes), 100°C (boiling); body temp = 37°C
  • Fahrenheit: 32°F (freeze), 212°F (boil); body = 98.6°F
  • Kelvin: 0K = absolute zero (-273°C); K = °C + 273
  • Conversion: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32; °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Body Temperature Regulation

  • Hypothalamus = thermostat; set-point = 37°C ± 0.5°C
  • Heat production: metabolism, shivering, brown fat (neonates)
  • Heat loss: radiation (60%), evaporation (22%), conduction (3%), convection (15%)
  • Fever: ↑ set-point (pyrogens — IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, PGE₂)

Heat for Sterilization

  • Autoclave: 121°C at 15 psi for 15 min (saturated steam under pressure)
  • Dry heat oven: 160°C for 60 min (glassware, oils, powders)
  • Pasteurization: 63°C for 30 min (LTLT) or 72°C for 15 sec (HTST)

Light
Reflection · Eye & vision · Biological effects · Phototherapy

💡
Light — Laws, Eye, Vision & Therapy
Light
👆 Click to expand notes

Laws of Reflection

  • Angle of incidence = angle of reflection
  • Incident ray, reflected ray & normal are coplanar
  • Refraction: bending of light at interface (Snell's law: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂)

Focussing Elements of the Eye

  • Cornea (major focusing): 2/3 of total refractive power (~43 D)
  • Crystalline lens: variable focus (accommodation) — 15–25 D
  • Vitreous humor & aqueous humor: maintain shape & refraction
  • Image formed on retina (inverted, real) → rods (dim light) & cones (color/detail)

Defective Vision & Correction

  • Myopia (short-sight): image in front of retina → concave lens
  • Hyperopia (long-sight): image behind retina → convex lens
  • Astigmatism: irregular cornea → cylindrical lens
  • Presbyopia: aging lens loss of elasticity → bifocal lenses

Energy, Frequency & Wavelength

  • E = hf (Planck's equation); c = fλ (f = frequency, λ = wavelength)
  • Visible spectrum: 400–700 nm; UV = <400 nm; IR = >700 nm

Biological Effects & Phototherapy

  • UV-B: vitamin D synthesis in skin; UV-C: germicidal (kills bacteria)
  • Phototherapy: blue light (430–490 nm) for neonatal jaundice — converts bilirubin to photoisomers
  • LASER therapy: ophthalmology (retinal photocoagulation), dermatology, surgery
  • Infrared: heat lamps for pain relief, physiotherapy
  • Excess UV: DNA damage → skin cancer, cataracts
Unit VII
Pressures
Atmospheric · Hydrostatic · Osmotic · Blood pressure · Intracranial
▼
🩸
Types of Pressure & Clinical Measurements
Pressure
👆 Click to expand notes

Atmospheric Pressure

  • 760 mmHg (1 atm) at sea level; exerted by weight of air
  • Used in: sphygmomanometer (BP measurement), altitude sickness understanding
  • Boyle's Law: breathing — ↓ volume → ↑ pressure; ↑ volume → ↓ pressure

Hydrostatic Pressure

  • Pressure exerted by a fluid at rest due to gravity
  • P = ρgh; at capillary: ~35 mmHg (arterial end) → 15 mmHg (venous end)
  • Drives filtration out of capillaries (Starling forces)

Osmotic Pressure

  • Pressure needed to stop osmosis; depends on solute concentration
  • Plasma oncotic pressure (COP): ~25 mmHg; maintained by albumin
  • Decreased albumin → ↓ COP → edema (e.g., liver failure, nephrotic syndrome)

Blood Pressures

  • Normal BP: 120/80 mmHg (systolic/diastolic)
  • Hypertension: ≥130/80; Hypotension: <90/60
  • Central Venous Pressure (CVP): 2–6 mmHg; monitors right heart filling

Ocular & Intracranial Pressure

  • Intraocular pressure (IOP): 10–21 mmHg; ↑ = Glaucoma
  • Intracranial pressure (ICP): 5–15 mmHg; ↑ = herniation risk
  • Nursing: monitor neuro signs (Cushing's triad: ↑BP, bradycardia, irregular respirations)
Unit VIII
Sound
Frequency · Velocity · Intensity · Ultrasound · Noise pollution
▼
🔊
Sound — Properties, Vocalization & Ultrasound
Sound
👆 Click to expand notes

Properties of Sound

  • Frequency (Hz): number of cycles per second; pitch perception
  • Velocity: 340 m/s in air; 1500 m/s in water (travels faster in denser media)
  • Intensity (Decibels, dB): loudness; logarithmic scale
  • Audible range: 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz (human ear)
  • Infrasound: <20 Hz; Ultrasound: >20,000 Hz

Vocalization & Hearing

  • Voice: larynx vibrates (20–2000 Hz fundamental); resonance in pharynx/sinuses
  • Hearing: sound → ear canal → tympanic membrane vibration → ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) → oval window → cochlea → CN VIII → brain
  • Normal hearing threshold: 0–25 dB (HL)

Ultrasound in Medicine

  • Frequency: 2–20 MHz for diagnostic imaging
  • Uses: fetal monitoring, cardiac echo, abdominal scan, DVT detection
  • Doppler ultrasound: blood flow velocity measurement
  • Therapeutic: physiotherapy (deep heat), lithotripsy (kidney stones)

Noise Pollution & Prevention

  • Harmful noise: >85 dB sustained; >140 dB = immediate damage
  • Hospital noise: >40 dB in ICU disrupts sleep & healing
  • Prevention: ear plugs, soundproofing, WHO guidelines for hospital noise
  • NIHL (Noise-Induced Hearing Loss): irreversible; audiometry screening
Unit IX
Electricity & Electromagnetism
Current · Voltage · ECG · EEG · EMG · Pacemakers · MRI · CAT Scan
▼
⚡
Electricity — Nature, Units & Human Body
Electricity
👆 Click to expand notes

Basic Concepts

  • Voltage (V): potential difference driving current; unit = Volt
  • Current (I): flow of charge; unit = Ampere
  • Resistance (R): opposition to current; unit = Ohm (Ω)
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR
  • Power: P = VI = I²R; unit = Watt

Flow in Different Media

  • Solids: free electrons carry current (metals); semiconductors (diodes, transistors)
  • Electrolytes (body fluids): ions carry current (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻)
  • Gases: ionized gas (plasma); fluorescent tubes
  • Vacuum: cathode rays (X-ray tube)

Electricity in the Human Body

  • Resting membrane potential: −70 mV (neurons), −90 mV (cardiac muscle)
  • Action potential: Na⁺ influx → depolarization; K⁺ efflux → repolarization
  • Threshold: ~−55 mV; All-or-nothing principle
📈
ECG, EEG, EMG, ECT & Pacemakers
Bioelectricity
👆 Click to expand notes

ECG (Electrocardiography)

  • Records electrical activity of heart; electrodes on skin
  • P wave: atrial depolarization; QRS: ventricular depolarization; T wave: ventricular repolarization
  • Normal PR interval: 0.12–0.20 sec; QRS: <0.12 sec
  • Uses: MI diagnosis, arrhythmias, electrolyte abnormalities

EEG (Electroencephalography)

  • Records brain electrical activity; scalp electrodes
  • Alpha (8–13 Hz): relaxed awake; Beta (13–30 Hz): active thinking
  • Delta (<4 Hz): deep sleep; Theta (4–8 Hz): drowsy
  • Uses: epilepsy diagnosis, brain death, sleep disorders

EMG (Electromyography)

  • Records electrical activity of muscles; needle or surface electrodes
  • Uses: motor neuron disease, muscular dystrophy, nerve conduction studies

ECT (Electroconvulsive Therapy)

  • Controlled seizure induced by brief electrical current to brain
  • Used for severe depression, catatonia unresponsive to medication
  • Nursing: consent, modified ECT with anesthesia, monitor vitals

Pacemakers & Defibrillation

  • Pacemaker: delivers electrical impulses to maintain heart rate (Brady-arrhythmias)
  • Defibrillator: delivers controlled shock to restore normal rhythm (VF, VT)
  • AED: Automated External Defibrillator — used by public in cardiac arrest
🧲
MRI & CAT Scan
Imaging
👆 Click to expand notes

MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

  • Uses strong magnetic field & radiofrequency waves — NO ionizing radiation
  • Principle: H⁺ atoms align with magnetic field → RF pulse → relaxation → signal detected
  • Best for: soft tissue (brain, spinal cord, cartilage, ligaments)
  • Nursing: remove all metal (implants, pacemakers — CI); claustrophobia — sedation
  • Contrast agent: Gadolinium (not iodine)

CAT Scan (CT — Computed Axial Tomography)

  • Uses X-rays from multiple angles → computer reconstructs cross-sectional images
  • Ionizing radiation — radiation dose concern; not in pregnancy
  • Best for: bone fractures, hemorrhage, chest/abdomen organs, emergency assessment
  • Contrast: iodine-based (check for allergy, renal function)
  • Nursing: allergy history, renal function (creatinine), NPO if contrast

Atomic Energy & Radioactivity

▼
☢️
Atomic Structure, Isotopes & Radioactivity
Nuclear
👆 Click to expand notes

Atomic Structure

  • Nucleus: protons (Z = atomic number) + neutrons; Electrons orbit in shells
  • Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons
  • Isotopes: same Z, different A (same element, different mass) — e.g., ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C
  • Isobars: same A, different Z — e.g., ⁴⁰Ca and ⁴⁰Ar

Radioactivity

  • Spontaneous emission from unstable nuclei; 3 types:
  • Alpha (α): Helium nucleus (2p+2n); low penetration; stopped by paper/skin
  • Beta (β): Electron emission; moderate penetration; stopped by aluminium
  • Gamma (γ): High energy EMR; high penetration; stopped by lead/concrete
  • Half-life: time for ½ of radioactive atoms to decay

Medical Use of Radioactive Isotopes

  • ¹³¹I: Thyroid scan & treatment (thyroid cancer, hyperthyroidism)
  • ⁹⁹ᵐTc: Most widely used — bone scan, cardiac scan
  • ¹⁸F-FDG: PET scan — cancer staging, brain activity
  • ⁶⁰Co: Cobalt therapy — cancer radiotherapy

Radiation Protection & Units

  • Roentgen (R): exposure; Gray (Gy): absorbed dose; Sievert (Sv): equivalent dose
  • ALARA principle: As Low As Reasonably Achievable
  • Protection: Time (minimize exposure), Distance (inverse square law), Shielding (lead aprons)
  • TLD (Thermoluminescent Dosimeter): personal radiation monitoring badge

X-Rays

  • Produced in X-ray tube: electron bombardment of tungsten anode
  • 0.01–10 nm wavelength; high energy photons
  • Uses: bone imaging, CXR, fluoroscopy, mammography
  • Contrast: barium (GI tract), iodine (blood vessels)
  • Hazard: ionizing — DNA damage, teratogenic; radiation protection protocols mandatory

Principles of Electronics

▼
🖥️
Electronics — Principles & Patient Care Equipment
Electronics
👆 Click to expand notes

Basic Electronic Components

  • Resistor: controls current flow (Ohm); Capacitor: stores charge (Farad)
  • Diode: allows current in one direction only (rectification — AC → DC)
  • Transistor: amplification & switching; basis of digital circuits
  • Integrated Circuit (IC/chip): thousands of transistors on silicon chip

Electronic Equipment in Patient Care

  • Cardiac monitor: Continuous ECG display; alarms for arrhythmias
  • Pulse oximeter: Beer-Lambert law; measures SpO₂ via light absorption (red & IR)
  • Infusion pump: Precise drug/fluid delivery; programmable flow rates
  • Ventilator: Electronic controls for RR, TV, FiO₂, PEEP
  • Defibrillator: Capacitor discharges shock for cardiac rhythm restoration
  • Diathermy (electrosurgery): High-frequency AC current for tissue cutting/coagulation
  • TENS unit: Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation — pain management
  • Infra-red thermometer: Detects IR radiation from tympanic membrane → temperature

Electrical Safety in Hospital

  • Microshock: <1 mA directly to heart → VF (ICU patients with catheters)
  • Macroshock: >1 mA through body; threshold for VF ~100 mA
  • Grounding/earthing: prevents leakage current
  • Isolated power supply in cardiac catheterization labs

🥗
Definition & Scope of Nutrition
Introduction
👆 Click to expand notes

Definition

  • Nutrition: Science of food & how nutrients in food are used by the body for growth, maintenance & repair
  • Dietetics: Application of nutritional science to planning & supervision of diets
  • Nutrient: Chemical substance in food that provides energy, supports growth/repair, regulates body processes

Classification of Nutrients

  • Macronutrients: Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats (provide energy — calories)
  • Micronutrients: Vitamins, Minerals (no calorie but essential functions)
  • Water: Universal solvent, transport, thermoregulation

Caloric Values

  • Carbohydrates: 4 kcal/g
  • Proteins: 4 kcal/g
  • Fats: 9 kcal/g
  • Alcohol: 7 kcal/g (no nutritional value)

RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance)

  • ICMR standards for Indians; varies by age, sex, physiological status
  • Adult male 2320 kcal/day; adult female 1900 kcal/day
  • Pregnant women: +300 kcal; Lactating: +550 kcal
Unit II
Carbohydrates & Fats
Classification · Sources · Functions · Deficiency
▼
🍚
Carbohydrates — Classification, Sources & Functions
Carbohydrates
👆 Click to expand notes

Classification

  • Monosaccharides: Glucose, Fructose, Galactose — simplest units
  • Disaccharides: Sucrose (glucose+fructose), Lactose (glucose+galactose), Maltose (glucose+glucose)
  • Polysaccharides: Starch (plants), Glycogen (animals), Cellulose (dietary fiber)

Dietary Sources

  • Cereals, rice, wheat, bread, potatoes, sugars, fruits
  • Fiber: vegetables, legumes, whole grains

Functions

  • Primary energy source: brain exclusively uses glucose
  • Protein sparing: adequate carbs prevent protein catabolism
  • Fiber: prevents constipation, reduces colorectal cancer risk, controls blood sugar
🥑
Fats — Classification, Sources & Functions
Fats
👆 Click to expand notes

Classification

  • Saturated fats: no double bonds; solid at room temp; animal sources (butter, lard)
  • Unsaturated: MUFA (olive oil), PUFA (fish oil, flaxseed)
  • Trans fats: artificial hydrogenation; most harmful to CV health
  • Omega-3 (α-linolenic): anti-inflammatory; fish, walnuts, flaxseed
  • Omega-6 (linoleic): pro-inflammatory in excess; vegetable oils

Functions

  • Concentrated energy (9 kcal/g); fat-soluble vitamin absorption (A,D,E,K)
  • Cell membrane structure, insulation, hormone synthesis
  • Essential fatty acids: linoleic & α-linolenic — deficiency → dermatitis, poor wound healing
Unit III
Proteins & Vitamins
Functions · Sources · Deficiency diseases
▼
🥩
Proteins — Sources, Functions & PEM
Proteins
👆 Click to expand notes

Dietary Sources

  • Complete proteins (all EAAs): meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy
  • Incomplete proteins: legumes, cereals — combine for complete profile
  • BV (Biological Value): egg = 100 (reference); milk = 91; beef = 80

Functions

  • Growth & repair; enzyme & hormone synthesis; immunity (immunoglobulins); transport (hemoglobin, albumin)
  • RDA: 0.8–1 g/kg/day; athletes: 1.2–2 g/kg/day

PEM (Protein Energy Malnutrition)

  • Kwashiorkor: Protein deficiency (adequate calories); edema, moon face, depigmented hair
  • Marasmus: Both protein + calorie deficiency; wasting, "old man face", no edema
💊
Vitamins — Fat & Water Soluble
Vitamins
👆 Click to expand notes

Fat Soluble Vitamins (ADEK) — stored in body

  • Vit A (Retinol): Vision (rhodopsin), epithelium maintenance; deficiency → night blindness, xerophthalmia
  • Vit D (Calciferol): Ca²⁺ absorption; bone mineralization; deficiency → rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults)
  • Vit E (Tocopherol): Antioxidant; prevents cell membrane oxidation
  • Vit K (Phylloquinone): Clotting factors II,VII,IX,X; deficiency → bleeding tendency

Water Soluble Vitamins (B complex + C) — not stored; daily needed

  • B1 (Thiamine): TCA cofactor; deficiency → Beriberi (wet/dry), Wernicke's encephalopathy
  • B2 (Riboflavin): FAD coenzyme; deficiency → ariboflavinosis (angular cheilitis)
  • B3 (Niacin): NAD⁺; deficiency → Pellagra (3Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhea, Dementia)
  • B6 (Pyridoxine): Amino acid metabolism; deficiency → peripheral neuropathy
  • B9 (Folate): DNA synthesis; deficiency → megaloblastic anemia, Neural tube defects
  • B12 (Cobalamin): Myelin synthesis; deficiency → pernicious anemia, subacute combined degeneration
  • Vit C (Ascorbic acid): Collagen synthesis, antioxidant; deficiency → Scurvy (gum bleeding, perifollicular hemorrhage)
🧠 Fat-soluble: "All Dogs Eat Kibble" — A, D, E, K

⛏️
Minerals — Macro & Trace Elements
Minerals
👆 Click to expand notes

Macrominerals (>100 mg/day needed)

  • Calcium: Bone & teeth; muscle contraction; RDA: 800–1000 mg/day; sources: dairy, green leafy veg
  • Phosphorus: Bone, ATP, nucleic acids; sources: meat, dairy, nuts
  • Magnesium: 300+ enzyme cofactor; muscle relaxation; sources: nuts, whole grains
  • Sodium: ECF osmolality; nerve function; excess → hypertension
  • Potassium: ICF cation; heart rhythm; sources: banana, potato, orange

Trace Elements (<100 mg/day)

  • Iron: Hemoglobin & myoglobin; RDA: male 17 mg, female 21 mg (menstruating); deficiency → IDA (pallor, fatigue, koilonychia)
  • Iodine: Thyroid hormone synthesis; deficiency → goitre, cretinism; sources: iodized salt, seafood
  • Zinc: Wound healing, immune function, taste/smell; deficiency → growth retardation, hypogonadism
  • Fluoride: Dental enamel protection; deficiency → dental caries; excess → fluorosis (mottled teeth, skeletal fluorosis)
  • Selenium: Antioxidant (glutathione peroxidase); deficiency → Keshan disease (cardiomyopathy)

Therapeutic Nutrition

🏥
Therapeutic & Modified Diets
Therapeutic
👆 Click to expand notes

Diet in Diabetes Mellitus

  • Low GI foods; complex carbs preferred; avoid simple sugars
  • Carbs 50%, Protein 20%, Fat 30%; high fiber (15–25 g/day)
  • Regular meal timing; avoid alcohol; weight management

Diet in Hypertension

  • DASH diet: low sodium (<2.3 g/day), high potassium, magnesium, calcium
  • Reduce saturated fat, alcohol; increase fruits & vegetables

Diet in Renal Failure

  • CKD: Low protein (0.6 g/kg), low phosphorus, low potassium, restrict fluid
  • Dialysis: higher protein (1.2 g/kg) to compensate losses

Diet in Liver Disease

  • Cirrhosis: high calorie, moderate protein; sodium restriction if ascites
  • Hepatic encephalopathy: restrict protein; branched-chain amino acids preferred

Enteral vs Parenteral Nutrition

  • Enteral (tube feeding): NG/PEG tube; preferred when GI tract functional
  • TPN (Total Parenteral Nutrition): IV route; used when gut non-functional
  • TPN components: dextrose, amino acids, lipid emulsion, vitamins, minerals
  • Nursing: check tube placement, flush lines, monitor blood glucose, weight, electrolytes
